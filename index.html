One MD step

  1. Compute the force on every particle from the derivative of the potential.
  2. Update the velocities and positions of all particles (velocity Verlet, Δt* = 0.005).
  3. Advance time by Δt and repeat. The result is a smooth trajectory r(t).

One MC trial

  1. Pick one particle at random and propose a random displacement of at most δ.
  2. Compute ΔU = U(new) − U(old). No forces needed.
  3. Accept or reject with the Metropolis criterion and repeat. The result is a chain of configurations, not a trajectory in time.

What they share: for a system in equilibrium that is ergodic, both methods give the same static averages (for example ⟨U⟩ or the density profile), because both sample the Boltzmann distribution. MD gets there through dynamics; MC through a Markov chain.

Lennard-Jones reduced units: σ = ε = m = 1, 8σ × 8σ box with periodic boundaries, rcut = 2.5σ, δMC = 0.25σ. Changing the temperature rescales the MD velocities once and is used immediately in the MC acceptance rule.
